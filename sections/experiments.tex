\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We corroborate the theoretical results of Section~\ref{sec:analysis}
through large-scale simulations of \CBSOMminus\ and \CBSOMplus\ across
eight network sizes and a full range of connectivity levels.  The goals
are threefold: (i) to confirm empirically that only $R$-region bias
functions support consensus; (ii) to quantify the joint effect of
network size and density on the consensus threshold; and (iii) to
characterize how memory amplifies or suppresses these effects.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Simulation Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

The simulator generates scale-free graphs by preferential attachment
with degree-distribution exponent $\gamma=2.5$, a value in the
empirically documented range for scale-free social
networks~\cite{Barabasi1999,Newman2003,Clauset2009}.
The parameter grid is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Network sizes:}
        $n \in \{10, 50, 100, 500, 10^3, 10^4, 10^5, 10^6\}$.
  \item \textbf{Density:} $1$--$9$ average neighbors per agent.
  \item \textbf{Bias types:} Unbiased ($\SOMminus$/$\SOMplus$),
        Confirmation, Backfire, and Authority
        (all under $\CBSOMminus$ or $\CBSOMplus$).
  \item \textbf{Runs per configuration:}
        $1{,}000$ ($n \le 10^3$); $500$ ($n=10^4$);
        $50$ ($n=10^5$); $10$ ($n=10^6$).
  \item \textbf{Convergence criterion:}
        opinion spread $\max_i B_i^t - \min_i B_i^t < 10^{-3}$;
        iteration cap $1{,}000$ rounds.
  \item \textbf{Initial opinions:} $B_i^0\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.}
The \emph{Consensus Rate} $\mathrm{CR}$
is the fraction of runs that converge; the
\emph{Mean Convergence Time} $\mathrm{MCT}$ is the average round
count over converging runs.  We report $\mathrm{CR}$ throughout.\footnote{All experiments were run on an Intel~i7-1255U machine with 32\,GB RAM and a 512\,GB NVMe SSD.}

% -------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Uniform Networks}
\label{sec:exp-uniform}

A \emph{uniform} network assigns the same bias type to every edge and
the same silence model to every agent.  This isolates the contribution
of each cognitive region before mixing is considered.

\paragraph{Memoryless dynamics ($\CBSOMminus$).}
Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap-uniform} (top row) summarizes the full
$(n,\text{density})$ grid.  Both $\SOMminus$ and $\CBSOMminus$
(Confirmation) transition from near-zero to near-$100\%$ consensus as
density increases; the Confirmation panel is always at least as light
as the $\SOMminus$ panel, confirming that $R$-region biases act as
\emph{consensus accelerators}.  The advantage is most pronounced at the
transition: at $n=10^6$ and density~4, $\CBSOMminus$ (Confirmation)
achieves $70\%$ versus $10\%$ for $\SOMminus$ (Figure~\ref{fig:curves}).
The Backfire panel is uniformly black (zero consensus at every scale and
density), and Authority is likewise zero except for negligible events in
a 10-agent network.  This pattern is consistent with
Theorems~\ref{thm:cbsom-consensus-n3} and~\ref{thm:cbsom-consensus-n2},
which guarantee consensus only for $R$-region biases.  We note, however, that at $n=10^6$ the sample
is too small (10 runs per configuration) to assert an absolute
dichotomy; these results should be taken as indicative.

The consensus threshold grows sub-linearly with $n$: density~2 suffices
for $n \le 100$, density~3--4 for $n \in [10^3, 10^5]$, and density~4--5
for $n=10^6$.  The rightward shift of the S-curve across scales is
visible in Figure~\ref{fig:curves}: as $n$ increases, the transition
from zero to full consensus requires one or two additional neighbors
per agent, and the saturation level approaches $100\%$ once the
threshold is crossed (in all observed runs for $n \le 10^5$, where
run counts are 50 or greater).

\paragraph{Memory-based dynamics ($\CBSOMplus$).}
Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap-uniform} (bottom row) contrasts sharply with
the top row.  For $n \le 100$, the Confirmation panel retains a faint
non-zero region at low densities (e.g., $19.9\%$ at $n=50$,
density~2), while $\SOMplus$ is already near zero.
For $n=500$, $\CBSOMplus$ (Confirmation) peaks at $4.9\%$; for
$n=1{,}000$ it falls to $3.8\%$ and is confined to densities $4$--$7$.
Beyond $n=10^4$ no memory-based configuration, including Confirmation,
achieves a single consensus run under any density.
Figure~\ref{fig:memory-penalty} makes this collapse visible: the
Confirmation advantage that sustains high consensus in the left panel
($\CBSOMminus$) dissolves entirely in the right panel ($\CBSOMplus$)
as $n$ grows.

\subsection{Non-Uniform Networks}
\label{sec:exp-nonuniform}

\emph{Non-uniform} networks assign different bias types to different
edges according to four mixed configurations (proportions refer to
fraction of edges):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)]  $\SOMminus+\CBSOMminus(\text{Conf.})$, $70\%/30\%$
  \item[(ii)] $\CBSOMminus(\text{Conf.})+\CBSOMminus(\text{Back.})$,
              $80\%/20\%$
  \item[(iii)]$\CBSOMminus(\text{Conf.})+\CBSOMminus(\text{Auth.})$,
              $60\%/40\%$
  \item[(iv)] $\SOMminus+\CBSOMminus(\text{Back.})$, $90\%/10\%$
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Rationale for the choice of proportions.}
The four configurations above were selected heuristically rather than
calibrated to empirically measured distributions of cognitive biases in
real social networks; to our knowledge, no such data are available at
the resolution required to assign per-edge bias types.
Instead, the proportions reflect the following design logic, which
builds directly on the uniform-network results of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-uniform} and on the observation---already present
in the foundational Spiral-of-Silence model of
Aranda et al.~\cite{aranda2024sound}---that consensus is structurally difficult to
achieve even in the absence of cognitive biases.
Because uniform Backfire and Authority configurations yield zero
consensus at every scale and density, pure non-$R$ configurations
provide no interesting boundary to explore; the relevant question is
how small a disruptive minority is sufficient to destroy consensus
that would otherwise form.
Configuration~(iv) ($10\%$ Backfire) is therefore a
\emph{contamination} scenario: it asks whether a marginal fraction of
adversarial edges can override an otherwise consensus-forming majority.
Configuration~(ii) ($20\%$ Backfire) raises the disruptive load while
keeping a receptive-resistant supermajority, probing the threshold
at which the $\mathrm{conf}(x)$-dominated updating can no longer absorb
the opposing force.
Configuration~(iii) ($40\%$ Authority) places the two regimes in
near-balance, testing whether hierarchical influence at substantial
scale is compatible with global agreement.
Finally, configuration~(i) ($30\%$ $\mathrm{conf}(x)$, $70\%$
unbiased) asks the complementary question: can a receptive-resistant
minority \emph{rescue} or amplify consensus in an otherwise
unbiased network that already struggles to converge at large scales?
The proportions were thus chosen to span a gradient from marginal
perturbation to near-parity, covering regimes in which consensus is
expected to be fragile, recoverable, or structurally precluded.
We acknowledge that heterogeneous bias distributions that vary by
agent role, community membership, or temporal dynamics may shift
these thresholds, and leave the calibration of proportions to
empirical social-network data as a direction for future work.

\paragraph{Memoryless dynamics.}
Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap-nonuniform} shows the results.
Configuration~(i)---an $R$-region majority---closely mirrors the
uniform Confirmation baseline: consensus reaches $97\%$ by density~3
for $n=500$, and the transition shifts rightward to density~5 only
for $n=10^6$.  This demonstrates that a $70\%$ $R$-region fraction is
sufficient to preserve consensus robustness even when $30\%$ of edges
carry no bias.

Configurations (ii)--(iv) collapse to near-zero consensus at every
scale.  At $n=100$, configurations (ii) and (iv) produce at most
$2\%$--$3\%$ consensus across all densities; for $n \ge 500$ consensus
is identically zero.  Remarkably, a mere $10\%$ share of Backfire edges
(configuration~iv) suffices to eliminate consensus that would otherwise
occur with probability approaching $100\%$.  The \emph{robustness
asymmetry} is thus highly directional: $R$-region edges act as
consensus catalysts, while non-$R$ edges act as consensus killers that
cannot be neutralized by a surrounding $R$-region majority.

\paragraph{Memory-based dynamics.}
In non-uniform memory-based networks, consensus is essentially absent.
The $\SOMplus+\CBSOMplus(\text{Conf.})$ $70/30$ mixture produces
sporadic events only for $n \le 100$ (at most $30\%$, density~4); for
$n \ge 500$ no run converges under any mixed memory-based configuration.
Memory thus amplifies the disruptive effect of non-$R$ edges to a
structural impossibility at scale.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_heatmap_nonuniform.pdf}
  \caption{Consensus rate for \emph{non-uniform} memoryless networks

\subsection{Relative Density}
\label{sec:exp-relative}

The experiments in Sections~\ref{sec:exp-uniform}
and~\ref{sec:exp-nonuniform} parameterize connectivity by an absolute
neighbor count $k\in\{1,\dots,9\}$, which represents markedly
different levels of social exposure as network size grows.
To assess whether the observed effects depend on this choice, we
complement those results with a \emph{relative-density} analysis in
which each agent's neighborhood is fixed to a fraction
$\rho\in\{10\%,\,25\%,\,50\%,\,75\%,\,100\%\}$ of the maximum
feasible degree $n-1$.
The $100\%$ condition is the complete-graph limit: every agent
interacts with every other.
Experiments cover $n\in\{100,\,1{,}000,\,10{,}000\}$ with the same
run counts and convergence criterion as Section~\ref{sec:exp-setup}.

Figures~\ref{fig:heatmap-rel-uniform} and~\ref{fig:heatmap-rel-nonuniform} summarize all four scenarios.

\noindent\textbf{Uniform memoryless (Row~1).}
$\SOMminus$ and $\CBSOMminus$ (Confirmation) achieve $100\%$ consensus
at every relative density and every tested scale, including the
sparsest configuration ($\rho=10\%$, $n=10{,}000$,
i.e.\ $\approx\!1{,}000$ neighbors per agent).
Backfire and Authority yield $0\%$ everywhere.
The all-white and all-black panels confirm that the bias-region
dichotomy is a property of the bias type alone, independent of
connectivity level.

\noindent\textbf{Uniform memory-based (Row~2).}
All cells are black for $n\ge 1{,}000$ across all configurations and
all relative densities, including the complete-graph limit
($\rho=100\%$).
The only non-zero values appear at $n=100$: $11.1\%$ and $0.9\%$
for Conf\,+\,Backfire (80/20) at $\rho=10\%$ and $25\%$
respectively, and $0.1\%$ for SOM$^+$\,+\,Confirmation (70/30) at
$\rho=10\%$; all vanish at $\rho\ge 50\%$.

\noindent\textbf{Non-uniform memoryless (Row~3).}
The SOM$^-$\,+\,Conf (70/30) column is entirely white: mixing a $30\%$
confirmation minority with an unbiased majority preserves $100\%$
consensus at every scale and every relative density.
The three disruptive mixtures annotate small non-zero values
($1.4$--$3.0\%$) only at $n=100$; for $n\ge 1{,}000$ they are
identically zero regardless of $\rho$.

\noindent\textbf{Non-uniform memory-based (Row~4).}
The entire row is black: zero consensus for all configurations,
all three scales, and all relative densities up to the complete graph.

Together, Rows~2 and~4 sharpen the memory-barrier conclusion of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-discussion}: the suppression of consensus under
$\CBSOMplus$ cannot be overcome by increasing connectivity, up to and
including full connectivity, confirming that the barrier is structural
rather than a threshold effect.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:exp-discussion}

Three empirical findings summarize the experimental evidence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Sharp bias-region dichotomy.}
    $R$-region biases (Confirmation) yield $\mathrm{CR}$ approaching
    $100\%$ at sufficient density for $n\le 10^5$ (run counts
    $\ge 50$); at $n=10^6$ (10~runs), all Confirmation runs converge
    at density~$\ge 5$, but the sample is too small to assert
    $\mathrm{CR}=100\%$ unconditionally.
    Non-$R$ biases (Backfire, Authority) yield $\mathrm{CR}=0\%$
    in all observed runs at every scale and density tested.

  \item \textbf{Sub-linear consensus threshold.}
    The critical density grows from $2$ (small networks) to $4$--$5$
    ($n=10^6$), spanning only $3$ units across eight orders of
    magnitude of network size.  Confirmation consistently reduces the
    threshold by one unit compared to the unbiased $\SOMminus$ baseline.

  \item \textbf{Memory as a structural barrier.}
    Memory-based dynamics ($\SOMplus$, $\CBSOMplus$) almost entirely
    suppress consensus: even Confirmation retains non-zero
    $\mathrm{CR}$ only at $n \le 1{,}000$, and in non-uniform
    settings an $R$-region majority cannot compensate for
    memory-induced path dependence beyond $n=100$.
\end{enumerate}
