\section{Conclusions and Related Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We introduced \CBSOMminus\ and \CBSOMplus, two extensions of the
Spiral-of-Silence opinion models of~\cite{aranda2024sound} that
incorporate cognitive bias functions into the belief-update rule.
We showed that, in the memoryless model, the Receptive-Resistant
region~$R$ preserves the structural properties of the base model: global
silence never lasts more than one round, and the extreme opinions
contract monotonically. For scaled linear biases, we proved consensus
on cliques of every size $n\ge 2$. Large-scale simulations, with networks
from $10$ to $10^6$ agents, complement these results. They show a
dichotomy between bias regions, a sub-linear growth of the critical
density with network size, and a consensus barrier introduced by
memory. To our knowledge, this is the first work that embeds cognitive
bias functions into a Spiral-of-Silence framework and gives formal
consensus guarantees for the resulting models.

The work closest to ours is that of Alvim et al.~\cite{Alvim2024}. It
introduces the taxonomy of bias regions used throughout this paper
(receptive-resistant, malleable, backfire, and insular), and proves that
continuous $R$-region biases guarantee consensus in the synchronous
DeGroot model on strongly connected graphs. Our
Theorem~\ref{thm:cbsom-consensus-n3} lifts this guarantee to the
Spiral-of-Silence setting, where the silence mechanism decouples from
the bias layer. Unlike~\cite{Alvim2024}, our consensus result is
restricted to cliques and to the scaled linear family $\mathcal{F}_\alpha$.
The psychological foundations
of the confirmation, backfire, and authority biases considered here are
discussed in~\cite{Aronson2010}.

The models \SOMminus\ and \SOMplus\ were introduced
in~\cite{aranda2024sound}. That work establishes consensus in $n$-agent
cliques for $n\ge 3$ and exhibits the period-2 oscillation on two-agent
cliques. Our Theorem~\ref{thm:cbsom-consensus-n2} shows that every
scaled linear bias $f_\alpha$ with $\alpha\in(0,1)$ breaks this
oscillation and restores consensus. In our simulations, the memory-based
model \CBSOMplus\ reaches consensus far less often than \CBSOMminus\ at
every scale. This mirrors the behavior observed for \SOMplus\
in~\cite{aranda2024sound}, and suggests that memory acts as a barrier
to consensus independently of the bias layer. The decoupling identified
in Section~\ref{sec:analysis-structure} explains why the guarantees
of~\cite{aranda2024sound} carry over to \CBSOMminus: the silence
criterion depends only on raw opinion proximity, so the bias layer does
not interfere with the conditions that produce consensus in the base
model. A comparable argument for \CBSOMplus\ remains open, because
memory couples historical opinion values with the current silence
decision.

Bounded-confidence models~\cite{Deffuant2000} let agents interact only
with those whose opinions lie within a confidence threshold. The
tolerance radius $\tau_i$ of our models plays a similar role, but it
governs whether an agent speaks, not whom it listens to. Agent-based
simulations have also been used to study the Spiral of Silence in
online settings~\cite{Sohn2022,Wang2013,Ross2019,Cabrera2021}. Our
experimental study follows this line, and complements it with formal
results for the clique case.

In future work, we plan to extend cognitive biases to the confidence-based
silence models of Gaona~\cite{gaona2024opinion}, which have not yet been
studied under cognitive bias. We also plan to study dynamic network
topologies, in which the influence graph evolves over time.
