\begin{abstract}
\noindent We introduce two DeGroot-based models of opinion formation in social
networks that combine cognitive biases with the Spiral of Silence:
$\text{CBSOM}^{-}$ (memoryless) and $\text{CBSOM}^{+}$ (memory-based).
A community of agents is represented as a weighted directed influence graph.
At each step, every agent passes its disagreement with each influencer through a
bias function (confirmation, backfire, authority, or insular) and updates its
opinion accordingly. It then decides whether to speak or to remain silent.
Silent agents exert no influence in $\text{CBSOM}^{-}$, whereas in
$\text{CBSOM}^{+}$ they keep influencing others through their last public opinion.
We prove that two properties of the memoryless base model are preserved under every
continuous receptive-resistant bias: global silence never lasts more than one round,
and the extreme opinions contract monotonically.
For the scaled linear biases $f_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x$ with $\alpha\in(0,1)$, we prove
consensus in cliques of every size $n\geq 2$. For $n=2$, this removes the period-2
oscillation of the base model.
Simulations on networks of up to $10^{6}$ agents show that confirmation bias
preserves consensus under memoryless dynamics, whereas backfire and authority
biases almost always prevent it. Memory suppresses consensus even when most
edges carry confirmation bias.
\end{abstract}
